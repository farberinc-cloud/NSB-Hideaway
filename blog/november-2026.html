Flamingo Follies Weekend on Flagler Avenue

November 2026 • Author: NSB Hideaway

November gives Flagler Avenue a different job. On Saturday, November 14, and Sunday, November 15, 2026, the 36th annual Flamingo Follies Arts & Crafts Show takes over the beach street. Artists and crafters from around the state set up outdoors. Saturday runs 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., Sunday 9:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., and admission is free. The city closes Flagler to vehicles from Peninsula Avenue east to North Atlantic Avenue, so those blocks in front of the ocean are for walking.

It is an easy weekend from Orlando, Lake Mary, Longwood, or Casselberry, usually 45 to 75 minutes. The Hideaway is a meticulously clean bungalow in historic downtown New Smyrna Beach, about a five-minute drive from that beach. You come home to a private saltwater pool, an outdoor soaking tub, a rain shower, and a gas fire table that no other guests share. The house was remodeled in 2019 and stays a dedicated vacation rental, with a 5.0 from guests who wanted quiet and care. If the air is cool, pool heating is available at an additional cost.

Stay for Flagler, Then Have the Pool to Yourself

A private pool vacation rental is what turns Flagler into a weekend from Orlando instead of a long day trip. You stay in historic downtown New Smyrna Beach, about five minutes from the avenue, and the outdoor living is already yours when you get back.

Saturday can start at the Farmers Market in Old Fort Park, about 210 Sams Avenue, typically 7:00 a.m. to 12:30 p.m. The park holds coquina ruins that historians tie to the town's founding, though they still debate what the building was meant to be. In 1768, Scottish physician Dr. Andrew Turnbull founded a colony here and named it New Smyrna for Smyrna, his wife's birthplace. He recruited settlers from Minorca, Greece, and Italy. The colony did not last. By 1777, many of the remaining colonists had left for St. Augustine.

The beach access for this weekend is Flagler Avenue Beachfront Park, at 201 Buenos Aires Street, on the east end of the avenue. It has a pavilion, restrooms, showers, and a walk onto the sand. Off-beach parking is $20 from 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. During the show the avenue is closed to cars, so park in the lots around the district and walk. What you get is a beach town's main street, not a resort strip: booths, open shop doors, and the Atlantic at the end of the block.

The private pool is how the day should end. The saltwater pool, soaking tub, rain shower, and gas fire table stay on the property, for the people who booked it. After the crowd, a meticulously clean bungalow and water you do not share is the reason to stay over. Pool heating can be added when you want the water warmer than the November air. Dinner is a short hop back downtown. The Garlic, at 556 E 3rd Avenue, opens daily at 4:00 p.m. Book seven nights and the stay includes a $200 gift card there.

A few house rules keep that quiet in place: no pets, no smoking, no parties, guests 30 or older, and the person who books stays on the property.

If November 14 and 15 are the weekend you want, reserve while the dates are open. Flagler on foot, sand at the end of the street, and a private pool in historic downtown New Smyrna Beach is a small inventory once that weekend is on the calendar.
